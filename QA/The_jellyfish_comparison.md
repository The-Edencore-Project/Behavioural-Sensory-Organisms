# ❓ Can a Behavioural OS Navigate Like a Jellyfish? — Q&A Mode
*Mode 1 — Clean, direct, grounded*

---

## ❓ **Q: Jellyfish navigate without intelligence. Could a Behavioural OS do the same?**
**A:** Jellyfish navigate using ambient cues, not cognition. A Behavioural OS works the same way — but vehicles require planning, which jellyfish do not.

---

## ❓ **Q: How do jellyfish actually navigate?**
**A:** They respond to:
- light gradients
- currents
- pressure
- turbulence
- chemical traces

They don’t plan. They drift and adjust.

---

## ❓ **Q: Does this count as navigation?**
**A:** Yes — but it’s reactive, not intentional. Jellyfish avoid thresholds rather than choose destinations.

---

## ❓ **Q: Is this similar to a Behavioural OS?**
**A:** Exactly. A Behavioural OS:
- senses
- drifts
- adjusts
- avoids
- stabilises

It never plans or predicts.

---

## ❓ **Q: So why can’t a Behavioural OS control a vehicle?**
**A:** Vehicles require:
- trajectory prediction
- hazard modelling
- path planning
- rapid decision‑making

These are cognitive tasks. A Behavioural OS is non‑cognitive by design.

---

## ❓ **Q: Could a Behavioural OS assist inside a vehicle?**
**A:** Yes. It could:
- sense cabin stress
- detect unsafe behaviour
- adjust environment
- warn the driver

But it cannot steer, brake, or navigate.

---

## ❓ **Q: What’s the key difference between jellyfish and vehicles?**
**A:** Jellyfish live in a slow environment. Cars do not. Drift‑based navigation works in the ocean, not at 70 mph.

---

## ❓ **Q: Does this limit the future of Behavioural OS?**
**A:** No. It defines its domain: ambient, domestic, slow environments where sensitivity matters more than planning.

---

## ⭐ **Summary**
- Jellyfish navigate through ambience, not thought.
- A Behavioural OS works the same way.
- Vehicles require cognition — therefore a Behavioural OS cannot control them.
- It can assist, sense, and stabilise, but never steer.

A Behavioural OS is a jellyfish, not a pilot.
