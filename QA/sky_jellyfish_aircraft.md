# ❓ Sky‑Jellyfish Aircraft — Q&A Mode
*Mode 1 — Clean, direct, grounded*

---

## ❓ **Q: Could a Behavioural Airplane treat the natural world as its rails?**
**A:** Yes. If its sensors are rich enough, storms, pressure fields, other planes, and altitude bands become gradients it reacts to — not things it thinks about.

---

## ❓ **Q: Would it need intelligence to do this?**
**A:** No. It would use reflex, not reasoning. Like a jellyfish, it responds to conditions without planning.

---

## ❓ **Q: How would it avoid storms?**
**A:** By sensing turbulence, pressure drops, wind shear, and moisture spikes. If any exceed thresholds, it shifts course. No interpretation — just reaction.

---

## ❓ **Q: How would it avoid other planes?**
**A:** Other aircraft become moving pressure zones. The Behavioural OS treats them as repulsion fields and adjusts automatically.

---

## ❓ **Q: How would it maintain altitude?**
**A:** Altitude becomes a comfort band defined by pressure, density, lift, and drag. It stays inside the band by constant micro‑corrections.

---

## ❓ **Q: Could it handle infinite scenarios?**
**A:** Yes — the same way biology does. Not by predicting them, but by reacting to gradients and thresholds.

---

## ❓ **Q: Does this make it autonomous?**
**A:** No. It’s sophisticated, not intelligent. It fits the sky instead of navigating it.

---

## ❓ **Q: What’s the key idea?**
**A:** The world becomes the operating system. The plane becomes a reactive organism.

---

## ⭐ **Summary**
- A Behavioural Airplane can treat storms, traffic, and altitude as rails.
- It reacts to gradients, not meanings.
- It handles complexity without cognition.
- It becomes a sky‑jellyfish: sensitive, adaptive, non‑thinking.
