# MODE 1 — Q&A  
## Edencore IoT Categories (Pronounceable Abbreviation Standard)

---

## Q1 — What is the purpose of this document?
**A:** To lock in the official, pronounceable, vowel‑containing abbreviations for all Edencore IoT categories. These abbreviations are used across subsystem logs, behaviour maps, naming conventions, and story references. This is the canonical taxonomy.

---

## Q2 — Why do the abbreviations need vowels?
**A:** Because vowel‑containing acronyms are readable, pronounceable, and believable.  
HVAC and SAC already worked because they contain vowels.  
The rest needed to match that readability standard.  
This avoids the “LGT / ENS / APU” consonant‑soup problem.

---

## Q3 — What are the final Edencore IoT categories and their abbreviations?

### **01 — HVAC**  
Heating, Ventilation & Air Conditioning  
The environmental backbone: airflow, temperature, humidity, pressure, noise, vibration.

### **02 — LUMA**  
Lighting & Ambient Systems  
All interior/exterior lighting, ambient strips, path lighting, mood lighting.

### **03 — SAC**  
Security & Access Control  
Locks, sensors, cameras, perimeter detection, entry/exit logic.

### **04 — ENVO**  
Environmental Sensors  
Temperature, humidity, CO₂, vibration, sound, pressure, particulate sensing.

### **05 — APLO**  
Appliances & Utilities  
Washer, dryer, dishwasher, fridge, boiler, pumps, heat pump cycles.

### **06 — MEDI**  
Media & Communication Devices  
Speakers, TVs, intercoms, tablets, notification systems.

### **07 — MOTA**  
Mobility & Assistance  
Blinds, curtains, automated doors, stair lights, lift aids, accessibility devices.

### **08 — NEMO**  
Neighbourhood Mesh  
Shared environmental signals, timing patterns, local activity, external pressure shifts.

---

## Q4 — Why these specific abbreviations?
**A:**  
- They contain vowels → readable and pronounceable  
- They are short → ideal for logs and diagrams  
- They are distinct → no overlap or confusion  
- They feel like real subsystem names  
- They map cleanly to physical behaviours  

This makes Edencore’s architecture feel engineered, not improvised.

---

## Q5 — How does this taxonomy help the Edencore system?
**A:**  
It gives Edencore a stable internal vocabulary.  
Every behaviour, event, and subsystem action can be tagged, logged, and referenced using these codes.  
This creates consistency across:

- behaviour maps  
- device IDs  
- system logs  
- story scenes  
- engineering documents  
- future expansions  

It’s the backbone of the entire house.

---

## Q6 — Are these abbreviations final?
**A:** Yes.  
This is the locked‑in, canonical Edencore IoT taxonomy unless you explicitly request a revision.

---

## Q7 — Can these abbreviations be used in naming conventions?
**A:** Yes — and they should be.  
Examples:

- `LUMA-01-HALL`  
- `HVAC-CORE-VENT3`  
- `SAC-DOOR-FRONT`  
- `ENVO-CO2-LIVING`  
- `APLO-FRIDGE-MAIN`  
- `MEDI-TV-LOUNGE`  
- `MOTA-BLIND-BED1`  
- `NEMO-PRESSURE-EXT`  

This creates a clean, believable engineering language.
