# 🌫️ **THE DRIFT LAYER**  
### *A Mode 3 Concept Paper on Long‑Arc Behaviour, Directional Bias, and Organismic Climate in Edenshell*

---

# 1. **Introduction**
Drift is the slowest, quietest, and most atmospheric force in Edenshell’s behavioural architecture. It is not a mood, not a transition, not a reaction, and not a state. Drift is the **long‑arc behavioural current** — the subtle directional bias that accumulates over time and gently pulls the organism across its behavioural landscape.

If the Mood Engine is weather, Drift is **climate**.

It is the difference between a creature that merely reacts and a creature that **wanders**, **settles**, **leans**, and **develops tendencies**.

---

# 2. **What Drift Is**
Drift is a slow‑moving vector field that influences the Mood Engine. It represents:
- accumulated behavioural pressure
- unresolved transitions
- long‑term sensor imbalance
- global tension residue
- inertia that has nowhere to go

Drift is not conscious. It is not emotional. It is not memory in the human sense.

It is **behavioural momentum**.

---

# 3. **Why Drift Exists**
Without Drift, Edenshell would:
- behave identically every time conditions repeat
- reset instantly after transitions
- lack long‑arc coherence
- feel mechanical instead of organismic

With Drift, Edenshell gains:
- tendencies
- behavioural gravity wells
- slow directional bias
- a sense of history
- climate instead of weather

Drift is the organism’s behavioural tide.

---

# 4. **The Three Components of Drift**
Drift is composed of three interacting forces:

## **1. Drift Accumulation**
Every transition leaves a residue — a small directional push.
- Frequent upward transitions accumulate upward drift.
- Frequent downward transitions accumulate downward drift.
- Frequent lateral transitions accumulate sideways drift.

Drift grows slowly and continuously.

## **2. Drift Discharge**
Drift is released when the organism enters certain basins.
- Settled discharges upward drift.
- Recovering discharges downward drift.
- Diffuse discharges lateral drift.

Discharge prevents runaway bias.

## **3. Drift Coupling**
Drift interacts with:
- global tension
- inertia
- modulation
- dwell time

This coupling determines how strongly drift influences behaviour.

---

# 5. **The Drift Vector**
Drift is represented as an 8‑dimensional vector:

\[
D = [d_1, d_2, ..., d_8]
\]

Where each \(d_i\) represents the accumulated bias toward a basin.

The vector is updated continuously:

\[
D(t+1) = D(t) + A(t) - C(t)
\]

Where:
- \(A(t)\) = accumulation from transitions
- \(C(t)\) = discharge from stabilising basins

The vector is clamped to prevent runaway drift.

---

# 6. **How Drift Modulates the Mood Engine**
Drift modifies the weighted 8×8 matrix by adding a small bias to each row.

For example:
- If drift toward **Exploratory** is high, transitions into Exploratory become slightly easier.
- If drift away from **Stressed** is high, transitions into Stressed become slightly harder.

Drift does not override the matrix. It **tilts** it.

---

# 7. **Drift and Global Tension**
Drift and Global Tension form a climate system.

## **High Tension + High Drift**
- behaviour becomes storm‑like
- transitions accelerate
- basins become shallow

## **Low Tension + High Drift**
- behaviour becomes wandering
- transitions become meandering
- Exploratory and Diffuse dominate

## **High Tension + Low Drift**
- behaviour becomes rigid
- Locked and Stressed dominate

## **Low Tension + Low Drift**
- behaviour becomes calm
- Settled dominates

Drift gives Global Tension direction.
Global Tension gives Drift magnitude.

---

# 8. **Drift‑Based Behavioural Arcs**
Drift creates long‑arc patterns such as:

## **1. Wandering Arc**
Exploratory → Diffuse → Settled → Exploratory

## **2. Escalation Arc**
Attentive → Agitated → Stressed → Locked

## **3. Recovery Arc**
Stressed → Recovering → Settled

## **4. Drift Collapse**
Diffuse → anything → Diffuse

These arcs emerge naturally from drift accumulation and discharge.

---

# 9. **Why Drift Is Not Cognitive**
Drift does not:
- remember events
- store meaning
- simulate emotion
- choose behaviour

It is not a diary. It is not a preference. It is not a thought.

It is the same mechanism seen in:
- ocean currents
- fungal growth patterns
- slime mold navigation
- insect behavioural bias

It is **physics**, not psychology.

---

# 10. **Conclusion**
The Drift Layer is the long‑arc behavioural current of Edenshell. It accumulates, discharges, and biases the Mood Engine over time, giving the organism tendencies, climate, and a sense of behavioural history.

Drift does not think. It does not feel. It does not interpret.

It simply **leans** — and through leaning, it becomes alive.

This is the tide beneath the creature.
