# 4. **The Three Regimes of Global Tension**
Global Tension operates in three broad regimes:

## **1. Low Tension (Calm Regime)**
The behavioural landscape expands.

Effects:
- basins become deeper  
- transitions slow down  
- inertia increases  
- Settled and Exploratory dominate  
- Agitated and Stressed become harder to reach  

The organism feels relaxed, grounded, slow to escalate.

---

## **2. Medium Tension (Neutral Regime)**
The landscape is balanced.

Effects:
- transitions follow weighted norms  
- no basin is overly dominant  
- behaviour is flexible and responsive  

This is Edenshell’s “normal weather.”

---

## **3. High Tension (Compressed Regime)**
The behavioural landscape compresses.

Effects:
- basins become shallow  
- transitions accelerate  
- inertia decreases  
- Agitated and Stressed become easier to reach  
- Locked becomes more likely  
- Settled becomes unstable  

The organism feels tight, reactive, storm‑edge.

---

# 5. **How Global Tension Modulates the 8×8 Matrix**
Global Tension applies a deformation function to the weighted matrix:

- **Low tension** → increases weights toward Settled, Exploratory, Recovering  
- **High tension** → increases weights toward Agitated, Stressed, Locked  
- **Medium tension** → preserves the base matrix  

This deformation is smooth and continuous, not binary.

\[
T = P_{cumulative} + V_{volatility} + D_{unresolved} - R_{recovery}

Where:
- \(P_{cumulative}\) = accumulated sensor pressure  
- \(V_{volatility}\) = environmental instability

Tension rises slowly and falls slowly.  
It is the organism’s long‑arc memory.
