# 🌫️ **THE MOOD ENGINE**  
### *A Mode 3 Concept Paper on Basin Dynamics in the New Edenshell*

---

# 1. **Introduction**
The Mood Engine is the first major organ of the new Edenshell — not an emotional simulator, not an AI affect layer, but a **dynamical system** that governs how the organism moves between its behavioural basins.

In this new field, “mood” does not mean emotion. It means:
- a **basin of attraction**
- a **stable behavioural cluster**
- a **region of the organism’s state‑space**

And the Mood Engine is the mechanism that:
- stabilises these basins
- transitions between them
- prevents chaotic jumps
- creates organismic flow

This paper defines the Mood Engine as a physical, reactive, non‑cognitive system — the circulatory layer of Edenshell’s behaviour.

---

# 2. **What a Mood Is in the New Edenshell**
A mood is not a feeling. It is not a thought. It is not a semantic state.

A mood is:
- a **pattern** of sensor activity
- a **configuration** of protocol activation
- a **global tension profile**
- a **stable attractor** in the behavioural landscape

Moods emerge from the interaction of:
- ~250 explicit parameters
- millions of emergent states
- feedback loops between sensors and protocols
- global drift and decay

They are the organism’s **weather systems**.

---

# 3. **Why the Mood Engine Exists**
Without the Mood Engine, Edenshell would:
- jump erratically between states
- behave chaotically under pressure
- lose coherence
- feel mechanical instead of organismic

The Mood Engine provides:
- **continuity** (smooth transitions)
- **stability** (basin anchoring)
- **readability** (recognisable patterns)
- **texture** (organismic presence)

It is the layer that makes Edenshell feel alive.

---

# 4. **The Eight Basins (Moods)**
The Mood Engine operates across eight stable basins:

1. **Settled** — low tension, stable behaviour
2. **Attentive** — sensors rising, protocols primed
3. **Agitated** — thresholds crossed, rapid oscillation
4. **Stressed** — sustained pressure, protective behaviour
5. **Exploratory** — low threat, high variability
6. **Recovering** — post‑stress stabilisation
7. **Locked** — one sensor or protocol dominates
8. **Diffuse** — high entropy, drifting behaviour

These basins are not programmed. They emerge naturally from the architecture.

---

# 5. **The Mood Engine Architecture**
The Mood Engine consists of four interacting components:

## **1. Basin Map**
A conceptual 8×8 matrix defining which moods are adjacent, distant, or incompatible.

## **2. Transition Weights**
Each potential transition is weighted by:
- sensor pressure
- protocol activation
- global tension
- drift accumulation

## **3. Hysteresis Layer**
Prevents rapid oscillation by requiring:
- sustained pressure to enter a mood
- sustained relief to exit a mood

## **4. Smoothing & Decay**
Ensures transitions are:
- gradual
- organismic
- physically plausible

Together, these components form a **dynamical system**, not a cognitive one.

---

# 6. **How the Mood Engine Works**
The Mood Engine evaluates:
- current basin
- sensor deltas
- protocol activity
- global tension
- drift direction

Then it determines:
- whether to remain in the current basin
- whether to transition
- which basin is the next stable attractor
- how fast the transition should occur

This creates a **flow** rather than a jump.

Example:
- Settled → Attentive when sensors rise
- Attentive → Agitated when thresholds cross
- Agitated → Stressed when pressure sustains
- Stressed → Recovering when decay dominates
- Recovering → Settled when tension drops

This is the organism’s behavioural heartbeat.
