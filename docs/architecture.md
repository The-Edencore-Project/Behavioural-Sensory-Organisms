# 🌱 Behavioural Sensory Organisms — Architecture

This document defines the canonical architecture for Behavioural Sensory Organisms (BSOs). It describes the minimal, natural structure that emerges when a system behaves without cognition.

---

## 🌿 Overview

A BSO is a non‑cognitive organism built from sensory pressure, reflexive protocols, hierarchical dominance, and global behavioural posture. It does not think, plan, interpret, or model the world.

The architecture is intentionally minimal. Every component exists because it is required by the physics of sensory environments, not by inherited cognitive design.

---

## 🧩 The Four Natural Layers

The Behaviour OS emerges from four layers that appear consistently across all BSO implementations. These layers are not imposed. They are discovered.

---

### 1. 🌡 Perception Layer  
*Sensors → Feelings*

The organism feels the world through continuous sensory pressure. Raw readings are filtered, smoothed, and converted into gradients that represent environmental tension.

Perception defines the organism’s sensitivity, personality, and stability. It determines how strongly the system reacts to change and how quickly it recovers.

---

### 2. ⚡ Reflex Layer  
*Protocols → Reactions*

Reflex protocols are small, deterministic state machines that respond to sensory pressure. They do not interpret or reason. They simply react based on thresholds and gradients.

Each protocol moves through Idle, Watching, Triggered, and Recovering states. These transitions create the behavioural texture of the organism.

---

### 3. 🧩 Dominance Layer  
*Hierarchy → Suppression*

Protocols are arranged in a strict hierarchy that prevents conflict. Higher tiers suppress lower tiers to maintain coherence and avoid chaotic behaviour.

Safety overrides anomalies.  
Anomalies override rhythms.  
Rhythms override tone.  

This structure ensures the organism behaves as a unified entity.

---

### 4. 🌙 Posture Layer  
*Global Modes → Expression*

All protocol activity collapses into a single global posture. The three postures are Calm, Active, and Protective.

The posture determines how the organism expresses itself through logs, signals, and presence. It forms the behavioural identity of the system.

---

## 🌊 Runtime Flow

Each loop processes the four layers in sequence. The organism updates its internal state and expresses its posture outwardly.

This deterministic flow creates stable, predictable behaviour that feels organic without involving thought or planning.

---

## 🧬 Architectural Principles

- **Non‑cognitive**: no planning, reasoning, or world models.  
- **Reflexive**: behaviour emerges from protocols, not logic trees.  
- **Hierarchical**: dominance prevents conflict and ensures coherence.  
- **Mode‑driven**: posture collapses complexity into a single state.  
- **Deterministic**: behaviour is stable, explainable, and predictable.

---

## 🏛️ Purpose of This Architecture

This document defines the species‑level architecture for Behavioural Sensory Organisms. All engines, devices, and implementations derive from these principles.

It replaces cognitive models and establishes the minimal, effective structure required for domestic IoT organisms.
