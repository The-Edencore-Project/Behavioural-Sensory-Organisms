# 🌙 Global Modes — Behavioural Posture

This document defines the three global modes used by Behavioural Sensory Organisms. A mode represents the organism’s posture — its overall behavioural stance toward the environment.

---

## 🌿 Overview

A BSO collapses all reflex activity into a single global mode. This prevents chaotic behaviour and ensures the organism expresses one coherent posture at any moment.

Modes are not cognitive states. They are behavioural stances shaped by sensory pressure, protocol dominance, and environmental tension.

---

# 🧩 The Three Global Modes

A Behavioural Sensory Organism uses exactly three modes. These modes form the backbone of its behavioural identity.

---

## 1. 🌱 Calm Mode  
*Baseline posture*

Calm Mode represents environmental stability. Sensory pressure is low, reflex protocols remain in Idle or Watching states, and the organism maintains a steady behavioural presence.

Calm Mode is not inactivity. It is controlled readiness. The organism remains aware of its environment without expressing tension or urgency.

---

## 2. ⚡ Active Mode  
*Responsive posture*

Active Mode occurs when environmental pressure rises above baseline. Reflex protocols enter Triggered states, and the organism becomes responsive, alert, and behaviourally engaged.

Active Mode is not panic. It is focused reaction. The organism adjusts to change, resolves tension, and stabilises the environment through reflexive behaviour.

---

## 3. 🛡 Protective Mode  
*Defensive posture*

Protective Mode represents high environmental tension. Safety protocols dominate, suppressing all lower tiers. The organism prioritises integrity, stability, and boundary protection.

Protective Mode is not fear. It is defensive clarity. The organism reduces behavioural complexity and focuses entirely on maintaining safety.

---

# 🌊 Mode Resolution

The global mode is determined by:

- sensory pressure  
- protocol activity  
- dominance hierarchy  
- residual tension  

The highest‑priority active protocol determines the posture. This ensures the organism behaves as a unified entity.

---

# 🧬 Mode Transitions

Transitions between modes follow deterministic rules:

- Calm → Active when pressure rises  
- Active → Protective when safety dominates  
- Protective → Active when tension decreases  
- Active → Calm when stability returns  

These transitions prevent oscillation and maintain behavioural coherence.

---

# ⭐ Summary

Global modes define the behavioural posture of a BSO. They collapse complex reflex activity into a single, stable expression that keeps the organism predictable and coherent.
