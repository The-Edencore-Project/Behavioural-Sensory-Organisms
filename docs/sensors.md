# 🌡 Sensors — Perception & Sensory Pressure

This document defines the perception system used by Behavioural Sensory Organisms. Sensors do not produce data. They produce pressure, tension, and environmental feeling.

---

## 🌿 Overview

Perception is the first layer of the Behaviour OS. It converts raw sensor readings into gradients that represent environmental tension. These gradients drive all reflex behaviour.

A BSO does not interpret or understand sensor values. It reacts to pressure, change, and instability.

---

# 🧩 The Six Canonical Sensors

These six sensors form the minimal anatomy of a Behavioural Sensory Organism. Together they provide complete environmental coverage without cognitive interpretation.

---

### 1. Motion Sensor  
*Disturbance Pressure*

Motion represents disturbance in the environment. It signals presence, movement, and rhythm. The organism reacts to motion as a form of environmental agitation.

---

### 2. Ambient Light Sensor  
*Circadian Pressure*

Light defines day–night cycles, brightness gradients, and environmental tone. It provides the organism with circadian structure and atmospheric context.

---

### 3. Temperature Sensor  
*Thermal Stability Pressure*

Temperature represents environmental stability. Rapid gradients indicate instability. Slow drift indicates long‑term environmental change.

---

### 4. Humidity Sensor  
*Comfort Pressure*

Humidity affects comfort, moisture levels, and potential anomalies. Sudden spikes may indicate leaks or environmental stress.

---

### 5. Vibration / Accelerometer  
*Structural Pressure*

Vibration represents structural tension, impact, and micro‑movement. It detects stress, instability, and environmental agitation.

---

### 6. Door/Window Contact Sensor  
*Boundary Pressure*

Contact sensors define environmental boundaries. Openings, closings, and transitions create pressure that affects safety and rhythm.

---

# 🌙 Sensory Processing Pipeline

Each sensor passes through a deterministic pipeline:

- filtering  
- smoothing  
- noise floors  
- hysteresis  
- gradient calculation  

This pipeline converts raw readings into stable, meaningful pressure signals.

---

# 🧬 Sensitivity Parameters

Sensitivity defines the organism’s personality. Each sensor includes:

- thresholds  
- noise floors  
- smoothing windows  
- gradient sensitivity  
- recovery rates  

These parameters determine how strongly the organism reacts and how quickly it returns to baseline.

---

# 🌊 Pressure Model

Sensors produce three forms of pressure:

- **Absolute Pressure** — the current environmental tension  
- **Gradient Pressure** — the rate of change  
- **Residual Pressure** — the leftover tension after recovery  

Protocols respond to these pressures, not to raw values.

---

# ⭐ Summary

Sensors provide the organism with environmental feeling. They do not produce data for interpretation. They produce pressure that drives reflex behaviour and global posture.
