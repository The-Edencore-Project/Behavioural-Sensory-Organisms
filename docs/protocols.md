# ⚡ Behavioural Protocols — Reflex Library

This document defines the 26 reflex protocols used by Behavioural Sensory Organisms. Each protocol is a deterministic state machine that reacts to sensory pressure without cognition.

---

## 🌿 Overview

Protocols are the behavioural core of a BSO. They convert filtered sensory pressure into reflexive reactions. Each protocol transitions through Idle, Watching, Triggered, and Recovering states.

Protocols do not interpret or plan. They respond to gradients, thresholds, and environmental tension. Their interactions are governed by the dominance hierarchy.

---

# 🧩 Tier 1 — Safety & Integrity  
*Highest priority. Suppresses all other tiers.*

### 1. Leak Detection Protocol  
Responds to moisture anomalies and rapid humidity spikes. Protects the environment from water damage.

### 2. Structural Stress Protocol  
Monitors vibration, pressure, and impact patterns. Detects structural instability or sudden force.

### 3. Thermal Anomaly Protocol  
Reacts to dangerous temperature gradients or overheating conditions.

### 4. Pressure Protocol  
Detects abnormal air pressure changes that may indicate drafts, breaches, or system faults.

### 5. Door/Window Protocol  
Monitors boundary integrity through contact sensors and motion adjacency.

### 6. Overload Protocol  
Responds to excessive sensory pressure or conflicting signals. Forces the organism into Protective posture.

---

# 🧩 Tier 2 — Anomaly & Drift  
*Overrides rhythm and tone. Suppressed by safety.*

### 7. Drift Monitoring Protocol  
Detects slow environmental changes that indicate long‑term instability.

### 8. Noise Tolerance Protocol  
Responds to persistent or escalating sound pressure.

### 9. Vibration Protocol  
Monitors continuous micro‑vibrations that may indicate machinery, traffic, or structural fatigue.

### 10. Threshold Response Protocol  
Triggers when any sensor crosses a defined sensitivity boundary.

### 11. Gradient Response Protocol  
Reacts to rapid directional change rather than absolute values.

### 12. State Transition Protocol  
Monitors transitions between environmental states and reacts to instability.

---

# 🧩 Tier 3 — Rhythm & Comfort

### 13. Daily Cycle Protocol

### 14. Occupancy Rhythm Protocol  
Responds to human presence patterns and movement cycles.

### 15. Noise Comfort Protocol  
Maintains comfortable sound levels by reacting to deviations from baseline.

### 16. Light Comfort Protocol  
Responds to brightness changes and maintains environmental tone.

### 17. Humidity Protocol  
Monitors comfort‑level humidity and reacts to deviations.

### 18. Temperature Protocol  
Maintains thermal comfort through gradient‑based reactions.

---

# 🧩 Tier 4 — Atmosphere & Tone  
*Lowest priority. Provides personality and environmental feel.*

### 19. Emotional Tone Protocol  
Responds to combined sensory pressure to create an overall environmental mood.

### 20. Atmospheric Support Protocol  
Maintains the organism’s ambient behavioural presence.

### 21. Material Response Protocol  
Reacts to surface contact, taps, or subtle environmental interactions.

### 22. Plumbing Protocol  
Monitors water flow patterns and pipe vibration signatures.

### 23. Light Pattern Protocol  
Responds to flicker, shadow movement, and ambient light texture.

### 24. Noise Pattern Protocol  
Reacts to rhythmic or patterned sound signatures.

### 25. Seasonal Protocol  
Tracks long‑term environmental cycles and adjusts baseline expectations.

### 26. Heuristic Forecast Protocol  
Predicts short‑term environmental shifts based on recent gradients. Non‑cognitive, purely pattern‑based.

---

## 🌙 Protocol Behaviour Model

Each protocol follows the same four‑state machine:

- **Idle** — no tension  
- **Watching** — tension rising  
- **Triggered** — reflex active  
- **Recovering** — returning to baseline  

This shared structure keeps the organism predictable and stable.

## ⭐ Summary

These 26 protocols form the reflex backbone of all Behavioural Sensory Organisms. Their interactions, governed by dominance and posture, create coherent non‑cognitive behaviour.
