# 🧩 Dominance — Hierarchical Suppression Model

This document defines the dominance hierarchy used by Behavioural Sensory Organisms. Dominance ensures the organism behaves as a unified entity rather than a collection of competing reflexes.

---

## 🌿 Overview

Dominance is the mechanism that resolves conflicts between reflex protocols. Higher‑tier protocols suppress lower‑tier ones, ensuring coherent behaviour under all environmental conditions.

Without dominance, the organism would oscillate, contradict itself, or express multiple behaviours simultaneously. Dominance prevents this by enforcing a strict behavioural order.

---

# 🏛 The Four Dominance Tiers

The hierarchy is absolute. A protocol in a higher tier always overrides any protocol in a lower tier. This structure mirrors biological organisms where survival signals override comfort or mood.

---

## 1. 🛡 Tier 1 — Safety & Integrity  
*Highest priority*

Safety protocols dominate all other behaviour. When any Tier 1 protocol is active, the organism enters a defensive posture and suppresses all lower‑tier reactions.

Safety includes leaks, structural stress, thermal anomalies, boundary breaches, and overload conditions.

---

## 2. ⚠️ Tier 2 — Anomaly & Drift  
*Overrides rhythm and tone*

Anomaly protocols respond to instability, drift, and persistent environmental tension. They override comfort and atmospheric behaviour but yield to safety.

These protocols stabilise the environment before rhythm or tone can resume.

---

## 3. 🌿 Tier 3 — Rhythm & Comfort  
*Maintains environmental stability*

Rhythm protocols manage daily cycles, occupancy patterns, comfort levels, and environmental drift. They operate only when safety and anomaly conditions are absent.

These protocols give the organism its sense of environmental continuity.

---

## 4. 🌫 Tier 4 — Atmosphere & Tone  
*Lowest priority*

Tone protocols shape the organism’s ambient presence. They provide personality, subtle reactions, and environmental texture.

Tone is always suppressed by higher tiers and only expresses itself when the environment is stable.

---

# 🌊 Dominance Rules

Dominance follows four deterministic rules:

1. **Higher tiers suppress lower tiers.**  
   A Tier 1 protocol always overrides Tier 3 or Tier 4 behaviour.

2. **Only one tier may dominate at a time.**  
   The organism cannot express multiple tiers simultaneously.

3. **Dominance is determined by the highest active protocol.**  
   A single Triggered protocol sets the behavioural tier.

4. **Recovery cascades downward.**  
   When a higher tier recovers, control returns to the next active tier.

These rules prevent oscillation and ensure behavioural clarity.

---

# 🔁 Dominance Flow

Each loop evaluates protocol activity:

- If any Tier 1 protocol is Triggered → Tier 1 dominates  
- Else if any Tier 2 protocol is Triggered → Tier 2 dominates  
- Else if any Tier 3 protocol is Triggered → Tier 3 dominates  
- Else → Tier 4 dominates  

This flow guarantees predictable behaviour under all conditions.

---

# 🌙 Interaction With Global Modes

Dominance determines posture:

- Tier 1 → Protective  
- Tier 2 → Active  
- Tier 3 → Active  
- Tier 4 → Calm  

This mapping collapses complex reflex activity into a single behavioural stance.

---

# ⭐ Summary

Dominance is the coherence engine of a BSO. It ensures that reflex protocols do not compete, that behaviour remains unified, and that the organism expresses a single clear posture.
