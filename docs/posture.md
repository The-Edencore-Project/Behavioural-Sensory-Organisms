# 🌙 Posture — Global Behavioural Expression

This document defines the posture system of a Behavioural Sensory Organism. Posture is the organism’s global behavioural expression, derived from dominance and environmental tension.

---

## 🌿 Overview

Posture collapses all reflex activity into a single behavioural stance. It prevents the organism from expressing multiple behaviours simultaneously and ensures coherent environmental presence.

Posture is not emotion, cognition, or intent. It is a structural output of the Behaviour OS.

---

# 🧩 Purpose of Posture

Posture provides three essential functions:

1. **Coherence** — the organism behaves as one entity.  
2. **Stability** — behaviour does not oscillate or contradict itself.  
3. **Clarity** — external systems can interpret the organism’s state.

Posture is the final layer of the Behaviour OS and the only high‑level state the organism expresses.

---

# 🌊 How Posture Is Determined

Posture is computed from:

- sensory pressure  
- protocol activity  
- dominance tier  
- residual tension  

The highest active tier determines the posture. This mapping is deterministic and cannot be overridden.

---

# 🧬 The Three Postures

A BSO expresses exactly three postures. These postures define the organism’s behavioural identity.

---

## 1. 🌱 Calm Posture  
*Environmental stability*

Calm posture indicates low sensory pressure and minimal reflex activity. The organism maintains awareness without expressing urgency or defensive behaviour.

Calm posture is the baseline state of a stable environment.

---

## 2. ⚡ Active Posture  
*Environmental engagement*

Active posture occurs when reflex protocols respond to rising tension. The organism becomes alert, responsive, and behaviourally engaged.

Active posture is not stress. It is focused environmental adjustment.

---

## 3. 🛡 Protective Posture  
*Environmental threat*

Protective posture represents high tension or safety‑critical conditions. Safety protocols dominate and suppress all lower tiers.

Protective posture reduces behavioural complexity and prioritises stability.

---

# 🧩 Posture Mapping

Posture is mapped directly from dominance:

- **Tier 1 → Protective**  
- **Tier 2 → Active**  
- **Tier 3 → Active**  
- **Tier 4 → Calm**  

This mapping ensures posture reflects the organism’s highest behavioural priority.

---

# 🔁 Posture Transitions

Transitions follow strict rules:

- Calm → Active when tension rises  
- Active → Protective when safety dominates  
- Protective → Active when tension decreases  
- Active → Calm when stability returns  

Transitions cannot skip states or reverse without recovery.

---

# 🛡 Posture Constraints

Posture imposes behavioural limits:

- only one posture may be active  
- posture cannot be overridden by protocols  
- posture cannot express cognition or intent  
- posture cannot generate language or dialogue  

These constraints preserve the organism’s non‑cognitive identity.

---

# 🌙 Posture Expression

Posture is expressed through:

- logs  
- signals  
- environmental presence  
- behavioural tone  

Posture is not expressed through:

- speech  
- text  
- commands  
- explanations  

Expression remains behavioural, not linguistic.

---

# ⭐ Summary

Posture is the global behavioural expression of a BSO. It collapses reflex activity into a single coherent stance, ensuring stability, clarity, and non‑cognitive behaviour.
